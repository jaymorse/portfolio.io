Trip Plan

Gunnison Week — Fly Fishing Plan

Sun Jul 26 – Sat Aug 1, 2026 · 30-mile radius of Gunnison, CO
The Situation

Low-water year. Flows are well under median for late July — fish are concentrated, spooky, and afternoon water temps push into the danger zone on the freestone and meadow streams. CPW has voluntary noon-to-midnight closures on Tomichi Creek (below Sargents), Cochetopa Creek (below the La Garita boundary), and the Lake Fork — water hit 78°F on Tomichi and Cochetopa in mid-July.

The upside: the Taylor is a dam-fed refrigerator, the upper Gunnison is holding low-60s, and the first half of the week brings monsoon clouds that knock temps down and turn bugs on. This is a fish-early week: on the water by 7, off the warm stuff by noon, tailwater or evening caddis after that.

The Spots

1. Gunnison Town Stretch — Van Tuyl / Redden SWA, Whitewater Park

Easy 0–3 mi · walk/bike from the house

Your backyard. The Gunnison River SWA plus the Whitewater Park give you miles of public wading through town — browns, rainbows, whitefish. At 332 cfs it wades easily and the evening caddis (5:30–8pm) is the headliner as water temp drops back to safe. Riffle-corner seams with a caddis dry or hopper-dropper along grassy banks. Zero-friction, post-bedtime option.

2. Almont & the Upper Gunnison (Hwy 135 corridor)

Easy 10 mi, ~15 min · roadside pullouts

A string of public easements and pullouts off 135 from the Almont confluence toward town. Classic riffle-run water, best freestone fish in the valley. Mornings: stonefly nymph trailing a PMD. Midday: hoppers and ants tight to the banks. Float-boat traffic midday; wade anglers own it before 9am.

3. Shady Island River Park

Easy 3 mi N on 135 · 2-min walk · day-use fee

County river park just north of town, the standard put-in for the town float. Easy bank/wade access, good caddis water. Gets boat-launch traffic mid-morning; fish it early or in the evening.

4. Taylor River Canyon (Almont to the dam, Hwy 742)

Easy access / Med wading 12–28 mi, 20–40 min · National Forest

The workhorse of your week. Dam-controlled at 226 cfs, cold top to bottom all day — fish here when everything else is too warm. Lower stretch has private checkerboard (respect signs); the canyon proper is mostly public FS with pullouts everywhere. The bottom is greased bowling balls — studs or a wading staff earn their keep. Caddis, yellow sallies, PMDs; hopper-dropper in the pockets.

5. Taylor C&R "Hog Trough" (below Taylor Dam)

Easy ~30 mi, 45 min · park-and-walk · PhD fishing

The famous half-mile below the dam: mysis-fed rainbows the size of your leg, in plain sight, ignoring you. 6x–7x, mysis patterns, midges 20–24, long leaders, and company — the most crowded water in the basin. Worth one morning for the spectacle. Ego optional; leave it in the truck.

6. East River at Roaring Judy Hatchery

Easy 14 mi, 20 min · flat walking · special regs

The main public window on an otherwise private river. At 70 cfs it's skinny and transparent — long leaders, downstream presentations, one bad cast per pool. Big browns live here; kokanee stage in fall. Flies/lures only; check the regs sign. Strictly a morning venue this week.

7. Cochetopa Creek (Hwy 114 south)

Easy–Med 20–28 mi, 30–40 min · ⚠ mornings only

Classic meadow hopper water with browns that eat like they mean it. Good access at the Coleman easement and the BLM rec site. But it's on the voluntary closure list below the La Garita boundary — noon cutoff, no exceptions. On the water at 7, hopper-ant, done by 11:30. Tuesday's cool cloudy morning is the day.

8. Quartz Creek (Ohio City / Pitkin)

Easy–Med 24–28 mi, 40 min · roadside near Pitkin

Small-stream antidote to Taylor crowds. Browns and brookies, mostly unbothered, in pocket water along the Pitkin road. A 3wt, a box of attractor dries (Stimulator, PMX, ant), and nobody around. Higher and cooler than the valley floor, so it buys you more of the day. Fish the public water up high near Pitkin.

9. Spring Creek (Taylor tributary, CR 744)

Easy–Med 18–24 mi, 35 min · forest access up the drainage

The local shop calls it one of the most consistent creeks in the area, and they're right. Cold, shaded, wild browns and rainbows, forest access most of its length once past the lower private. Perfect storm-dodging option — short casts, quick exits.

10. Blue Mesa Reservoir (Iola basin, shore access)

Easy 10–25 mi W on Hwy 50 · park-and-walk shoreline

Wildcard, and the toddler-compatible option: shoreline access at Iola and points west, browns and rainbows cruising the banks early and late, kokanee out deep. Streamer or a big attractor dry off the points at dawn. Down badly this year, so expect long mudflats at some access points.

Skips, and why

Pass this week

Tomichi Creek — 19 cfs and 64°F before lunch, closure below Sargents. Torturing heat-stressed fish isn't sport. Ohio Creek valley — almost entirely private ranch; public water only way up near Ohio Pass, small fish. Lake Fork at Gateview — edge of the radius, historically low and on the closure list.

Fly Box for the Week
Dries: Elk Hair / Corn-fed Caddis 14–16, PMD sparkle dun 16–18, Yellow Sally 14–16, hoppers (tan/gold) 8–12, cinnamon ants 14–16, PMX/Stimulator for the creeks.
Nymphs: Tungsten Pheasant Tail 14–18, Pat's Rubber Legs 8–10, caddis pupa 14–16, Perdigon 16–18.
Hog Trough only: Mysis 16–18, midges 20–24, 6x–7x fluoro.
Storm-cloud sleeper: a few BWOs 18–20 — monsoon overcast can pop an olive hatch out of nowhere.
